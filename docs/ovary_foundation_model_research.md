# Ovary gap favors narrow, Arc-scored models

As of 3 November 2026 the single-cell foundation-model (scFM) field has settled into a qualified consensus: generalist models (scGPT, Geneformer V2, UCE, TranscriptFormer, scPRINT-2, Tahoe-x1, Arc STATE) help on specific tasks — perturbation-response prediction when in-context perturbation data exist, low-supervision annotation, and gene-level embeddings — while independent 2026 benchmarks (VCBench, scTOP, DenAdel et al. in *Nature Methods*, the zero-shot and real-world-integration benchmarks) show that PCA, scVI and linear baselines still match or beat them on embedding, integration, trajectory and spatial tasks, and that pretraining scale saturates early ("much pain with little gain"). Against that backdrop the "ovary gap" is real and is now measured rather than asserted: in the newest CELLxGENE Census (LTS 2025-11-08; no 2026 build exists in the public bucket) human ovary is **373,467 unique cells, 0.375 % of the corpus, rank 31 of 71 tissues, and 53 % of those cells are ovarian-cancer cells**; mouse ovary is **17,408 cells, 0.083 %, rank 27 of 36, from two studies, with zero nuclei, zero spatial observations and nothing older than 9 months**; Tahoe-100M's 50 cell lines contain **no ovarian line**; ovary is **≈0.3 % of scBaseCount** in both species; no scFM paper reports an ovary count; and no single-cell foundation model exists for any reproductive tissue. The gap is one of curation rather than generation, because well over a million mouse ovary cells and spots and more than half a million human ovary nuclei (a 439,011-nucleus postmenopausal atlas, a 610,620-spot Slide-seq cycling/ageing atlas, the Morris, Mantri, Wang/Suh and McKey datasets) sit in GEO and SRA outside Census. For a small academic group with ~50,000 mouse ovary nuclei, matched Visium HD and A100 time on Biowulf, this implies a specific plan: do not train a large model from scratch; assemble a curated ~1–2 million-cell ovary corpus with a Cell Ontology-aligned label set; continue-pretrain or train a ≤100 M-parameter mouse-native model from a permissively licensed base that ships training code (scPRINT-2 under GPL-3, Tahoe-x1 under Apache-2.0, scGPT under MIT) against pre-registered scVI/scANVI and linear baselines; and adopt the Virtual Cell Challenge 2026 evaluation contract — six `cell-eval2` metrics, each rescaled so that 0 equals the context-mean baseline and 1 equals a split-half biological replicate — as the house standard for held-out-context prediction, using public hCG, ageing and knockout datasets plus the group's own oophorectomy data as held-out contexts. The honest differentiator for an ovary model is condition and perturbation coverage that the generalists never saw, not tissue restriction in itself.

**How to read the evidence tags in this report.** The six research notes were compiled on 2026-11-03 in a sandbox where nature.com, cell.com, bioRxiv, arXiv, PMC, Hugging Face, arcinstitute.org, virtualcellchallenge.org and all cziscience.com hosts were blocked, so each claim below carries one of four tags: **[M]** measured in-session by opening the public CELLxGENE Census SOMA object on S3 and the Arc Institute GCS bucket (machine-readable tables are in `research_notes/Ovary foundation model gap/data/`); **[V]** verified from a primary file read in full (GitHub README, LICENSE, config or source, PyPI metadata, Cell Ontology issue pages); **[S]** taken from a search-engine snippet or secondary summary of a page that could not be opened, which should be re-checked against the paper before being quoted in a grant; and **[I]** an inference by the researchers or by this report. Section 7 separates the three classes explicitly.

## Generalists now win narrowly, and only on tasks with in-context signal

The open scFM landscape in November 2026 spans roughly 50 M to 3 B parameters and 30 M to 350 M pretraining cells, and nearly every 2025–2026 model is some mixture of the same three corpora: CELLxGENE (~60 M human and mouse cells), Arc's scBaseCount (**>502 million cells, 27 organisms, 75 tissues** in the 2026-01-12 release [V]) and Tahoe-100M (**100,648,790 cells, 1,344 samples** [V]) ([scBaseCount README](https://github.com/ArcInstitute/arc-virtual-cell-atlas/blob/main/scBaseCount/README.md); [Tahoe-100M README](https://github.com/ArcInstitute/arc-virtual-cell-atlas/blob/main/tahoe-100M/README.md)). Flagship models were published in top journals this year — UCE in *Nature* (July 2026), STATE in *Cell* (online 31 August 2026), TranscriptFormer in *Science*, Geneformer V2 in *Nature Computational Science* — and the largest entrants are scPRINT-2 (>350 M cells, 22,000 datasets, 16 species [V]), TranscriptFormer TF-Metazoa (112 M cells, 12 species, 444 M trainable + 633 M frozen parameters [V]), Tahoe-x1 (3 B parameters on 266 M profiles [V]) and Xaira's X-Cell, which is described as scaling to 4.9 B parameters [S] ([UCE](https://github.com/snap-stanford/UCE); [Nature s41586-026-10689-z](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-026-10689-z); [STATE](https://github.com/ArcInstitute/state); [Cell STATE paper](https://www.cell.com/cell/fulltext/S0092-8674(26)00921-9); [TranscriptFormer](https://github.com/czi-ai/transcriptformer); [scPRINT-2](https://github.com/cantinilab/scPRINT-2); [Tahoe-x1](https://github.com/tahoebio/tahoe-x1); [X-Cell](https://github.com/Xaira-Therapeutics/X-Cell); [GEN on X-Cell](https://www.genengnews.com/topics/artificial-intelligence/xairas-first-virtual-cell-model-is-largest-to-date-toward-complex-biology/)). Licensing has drifted from MIT (scGPT, UCE, TranscriptFormer, CancerFoundation) toward non-commercial terms (STATE, CellFM CC-BY-NC-ND, GB.Cell community licence, X-Cell CC-BY-NC-SA), a shift that matters later for choosing a base model [V] ([scGPT](https://github.com/bowang-lab/scGPT); [CellFM](https://github.com/biomed-AI/CellFM); [GB.Cell-100M](https://huggingface.co/genbio-ai/GB.Cell-100M)).

The strongest evidence *against* scFMs as general-purpose replacements for standard pipelines is independent and converges from several directions. DenAdel et al. (*Nature Methods* 23:1447–1457, 2026) pretrained 400 models across five architectures on subsets of the 22.2 M-cell scTab corpus and found that "current methods tend to plateau in performance with pre-training datasets that are only a fraction of the size" and "do not benefit from increasing pre-training dataset size or from naive approaches to increasing pre-training dataset diversity"; the accompanying Research Briefing is titled "Scaling up training dataset size for transcriptomic AI models is much pain with little gain" [S] ([Nature Methods s41592-026-03120-y](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41592-026-03120-y); [Research Briefing s41592-026-03119-5](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41592-026-03119-5); [bioRxiv 2024.12.13.628448](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2024.12.13.628448.full.pdf); [Broad summary](https://www.broadinstitute.org/publications/broad1378181); [PubMed 42265212](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42265212/); [microsoft/scFM-dataselection](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/microsoft/scFM-dataselection/main/README.md)). VCBench evaluated five scFMs including UCE, TranscriptFormer and STATE against pre-registered linear and nearest-neighbour baselines and found the **baselines matched or exceeded every foundation model on four of five dimensions; per tissue, PCA+kNN beat every model on lung, heart, kidney and brain**, with TranscriptFormer edging it only on liver (0.495) [S] ([VCBench](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.64898/2026.06.18.733146v1.full.pdf)). Souza and Mehta's parameter-free linear method scTOP reports "consistently higher macro F1 than foundation models" across eight species and on out-of-distribution cell types and organisms, running on a laptop CPU, with the caveat that it compares against published numbers rather than re-runs [S] ([arXiv 2602.16696](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.16696); [PMC12918925](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12918925/)). Kedzierska et al. (*Genome Biology* 2025) showed zero-shot Geneformer and scGPT underperform HVG+Harmony and scVI on clustering and fail to correct technical batch effects [S] ([Genome Biology s13059-025-03574-x](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s13059-025-03574-x)), and four further 2026 preprints reach the same conclusion for real-world integration (scVI best overall), zero-shot annotation, trajectory inference (HVG-PCA more reliable) and spatial-domain recovery (spatially native representations better) [S] ([integration benchmark](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.64898/2026.04.17.719314v1); [zero-shot benchmark](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.64898/2026.08.03.739553v1.full); [dynamics benchmark](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.64898/2026.03.10.710748v1.full); [unified deployment framework](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.64898/2026.01.06.698060v2.full); [PMC12803055](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12803055)). The harmonised UNSW benchmark of six models found "no universal winner" and that continual pretraining produced "strongly architecture- and dataset-dependent effects", with scGPT-spatial gaining only NMI 0.63→0.66 and silhouette 0.14→0.19 [S] ([arXiv 2607.17227](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.17227)). An Oxford-hosted analysis likewise "found that proposed single-cell foundation models fail to outperform cell embeddings derived from HVG or generated using the scVI model" [S] ([ORA record](https://ora.ox.ac.uk/objects/uuid:014a1035-7f2a-4c31-a680-6809eeb575a3/files/r8s45qb64r)).

The strongest evidence *for* scFMs is concentrated in perturbation prediction with adequate in-context data, in low-supervision annotation and in gene-level tasks, and it is disproportionately developer-authored. CellBench-LS counted how many of seven foundation models beat the strongest classical baseline per task: **clustering 1/7, batch correction 3/7, annotation 4/7, expression reconstruction 0/7, perturbation prediction 6/7**, and found that cross-model gene-relation agreement did not exceed a random-projection null [S] ([CellBench-LS v1](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.64898/2026.04.01.714123v1); [v2](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.64898/2026.04.01.714123v2)). GenBio AI's "Foundation Models Improve Perturbation Response Prediction" analysed >600 models and reports that several scFMs "meaningfully improve predictions for both genetic and chemical perturbations", that GEARS, latent diffusion and flow matching underperform them, and that "with sufficient data, these models approach fundamental performance limits" — authored by a model developer [S] ([bioRxiv 2026.02.18.706454](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.64898/2026.02.18.706454v1); [genbio-ai/foundation-models-perturbation](https://github.com/genbio-ai/foundation-models-perturbation)). A *Nature Biotechnology* 2026 paper argues deep perturbation models can beat baselines on calibrated metrics, but its evidence concerns unseen genetic perturbations, not unseen contexts [S] ([Nature Biotechnology s41587-026-03307-w](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41587-026-03307-w)). Arc's STATE is the main peer-reviewed claim of beating linear baselines in a held-out-context setting, with a perturbation-discrimination improvement of **63 % on Tahoe-100M, 47 % on Parse-PBMC and only 5 % on Replogle-Nadig** in the preprint, a pattern consistent with chemical responses across 50 lines being far easier to transfer than genetic responses across four [S] ([STATE bioRxiv v2](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2025.06.26.661135v2.full.pdf); [v1](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2025.06.26.661135v1); [Arc STATE news](https://arcinstitute.org/news/virtual-cell-model-state); [Semantic Scholar](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/Predicting-cellular-responses-to-perturbation-with-Adduri-Gautam/e52d2798ad225ca6a4702239f92230f7c20b016d); [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0092867426009219)). On the scaling question, developers continue to report gains (Geneformer V2 from 38 M to 316 M parameters; C2S-Scale from 410 M to 27 B; Tahoe-x1 at 3 B) [S] ([Nature Comp Sci s43588-026-00972-4](https://www.nature.com/articles/s43588-026-00972-4); [C2S-Scale preprint](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2025.04.14.648850v2.full.pdf); [cell2sentence](https://github.com/vandijklab/cell2sentence); [C2S-Scale-Gemma-2-27B](https://huggingface.co/vandijklab/C2S-Scale-Gemma-2-27B); [Google Research post](https://x.com/GoogleResearch/status/1978509364504744264)), and a capacity-bottleneck rebuttal argues the null results reflect undersized models rather than the absence of a law [S] ([arXiv 2608.02961](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.02961)); but the most striking 2026 datum points the other way: a **70 M-parameter Tahoe-x1 continued-pretrained on proteomics matched or beat the 1 B and 3 B RNA-only models** on most of Tahoe's own benchmarks ("adding proteomics data beats parameter scaling") [S] ([bioRxiv 2026.08.14.744845](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.64898/2026.08.14.744845v1)).

Tissue- and disease-specific pretraining has a thinner evidence base than its popularity suggests. The specialised models that exist are CancerFoundation (~1 M malignant cells, MIT) [V], the Geneformer-V2 cancer variant continually pretrained on ~14 M cancer cells [S/V], evoCancerGPT (2.76 M cells from scratch) [V], EVA for immuno-inflammation (50 M parameters) [S], Nephrobase Cell+ for kidney (pretrained from scratch on ~39.5 M human, mouse, rat and pig cells and nuclei) [S], BrainBeacon for brain spatial data (144 M observations, four species) [V/S], scGPT's organ checkpoints (brain 13.2 M, blood 10.3 M, heart 1.8 M, lung 2.1 M, kidney 814 K) [V] and scGPT-spatial (30 M spots) [V] ([CancerFoundation](https://github.com/BoevaLab/CancerFoundation); [CancerFoundation bioRxiv](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2024.11.01.621087.full.pdf); [RWTH summary](https://dbis.rwth-aachen.de/dbis/index.php/2024/single-cell-centric-biomedical-foundation-models-for-cancer/); [Geneformer HF](https://huggingface.co/ctheodoris/Geneformer); [evoCancerGPT](https://github.com/cristea-lab/evoCancerGPT_main); [EVA](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2025.05.02.651839v1); [Nephrobase Cell+](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2509.26223); [PubMed 41256511](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41256511/); [BrainBeacon](https://github.com/ChengmingZhang-CAS/BrainBeacon); [BrainBeacon bioRxiv](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2025.07.08.663729v1); [scGPT-spatial](https://github.com/bowang-lab/scGPT-spatial)). The only published numbers that isolate "same model with versus without domain continued pretraining" come from an independent cancer benchmark: **Geneformer-V2[cancer] ≈0.86 ± 0.16 versus generic V2 0.81 ± 0.18** across seven tasks, with the authors noting that continual training "provided an impactful way to enhance the performance of smaller models" but only when combined with a thoughtful aggregation strategy [S] ([PMC12637420](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12637420/); [bioRxiv 2025.10.31.685892](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2025.10.31.685892v1.full.pdf)). Nicheformer's one-tissue anecdote — extended pretraining on liver improved liver performance, "suggesting undertrained tissues can benefit from additional fine-tuning" — is the only direct hint that an undertrained tissue is a good target [S] ([Nicheformer Nature Methods](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41592-025-02814-z); [theislab/nicheformer](https://github.com/theislab/nicheformer)). No study was found that pretrains a generalist and a tissue-restricted model of matched size on matched compute, and the curated OmicsML and MohammadUsman0 catalogues list no kidney-, heart-, retina-, liver-, blood- or reproductive-tissue scFM beyond those named here [V] ([OmicsML list](https://github.com/OmicsML/awesome-foundation-model-single-cell-papers); [raw README](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/OmicsML/awesome-foundation-model-single-cell-papers/main/README.md); [MohammadUsman0 catalogue](https://github.com/MohammadUsman0/single-cell-foundation-models)). Adaptation tooling has matured: scPEFT (*Nature Machine Intelligence* 2025) adds LoRA, prefix and gene-token adapters to scGPT, scBERT, Geneformer and scFoundation with ">96 %" fewer tuned parameters and claims gains in "disease-specific, cross-species, and under-characterized cell population tasks" [V/S] ([scPEFT](https://github.com/coffee19850519/scPEFT); [NMI s42256-025-01170-z](https://www.nature.com/articles/s42256-025-01170-z); [laolintou/scPEFT README](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/laolintou/scPEFT/main/README.md); [PMC12045372](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC12045372/); [He 2024 bioRxiv](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2024.01.27.577455.full.pdf); [PyPI scpeft](https://pypi.org/pypi/scpeft/json)), and PertAdapt (*Bioinformatics* 2026) targets perturbation adaptation [S] ([PertAdapt](https://github.com/BaiDing1214/PertAdapt)).

For reproductive tissue the answer is unambiguous. GitHub repository searches for `ovary "foundation model" single-cell` and `(ovary OR ovarian) (scGPT OR Geneformer OR scFoundation OR "foundation model")` return **zero repositories**; a REST-API search on 3 November 2026 found `ovary scGPT` = 0, `ovary Geneformer` = 0, `ovary "virtual cell"` = 0 and `ovarian "foundation model"` = 4, all of them histopathology or ultrasound imaging repositories; the OmicsML README contains no entry mentioning ovary, ovarian, testis, placenta, uterus, endometrium, gonad, oocyte, granulosa or trophoblast [V] ([GitHub search 1](https://github.com/search?q=ovary+%22foundation+model%22+single-cell&type=repositories); [GitHub search 2](https://github.com/search?q=%28ovary+OR+ovarian%29+%28scGPT+OR+Geneformer+OR+scFoundation+OR+%22foundation+model%22%29&type=repositories); [GitHub search 3](https://github.com/search?q=%28placenta+OR+testis+OR+endometrium+OR+uterus+OR+gonad%29+%22foundation+model%22+scRNA&type=repositories); [scjjb/Ovarian_Features](https://github.com/scjjb/Ovarian_Features)). Reproductive tissue appears in the scFM literature only as held-out evaluation data (scVision uses an ovary atlas for zero-shot testing) or as an unquantified slice of generalist corpora [S] ([scVision arXiv 2607.14163](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.14163v1); [HF scFoundation mirror](https://huggingface.co/genbio-ai/scFoundation); [HF collection](https://huggingface.co/collections/shenbaba/single-cell-multiomic-multimodal-foundation-models); [PMC12586647](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC12586647/); [AJOG expert review](https://www.ajog.org/article/S0002-9378(24)00650-1/fulltext); [stFormer](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2024.09.27.615337.full.pdf); [theislab transformer list](https://awesome.ecosyste.ms/projects/github.com%2Ftheislab%2Fsingle-cell-transformer-papers); [PEFT PMC10862733](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10862733/)). The only company claim — Gameto's "Deovo" computational platform for ovarian ageing — is marketing with no model, preprint or code [S] ([Nucleus on Gameto](https://mynucleus.com/blog/gameto); [Diamandis on Gameto](https://www.diamandis.com/blog/gameto-the-future-of-reproductive-health)). The caveat is that bioRxiv, Hugging Face and conference abstract books could not be queried directly, so an ovary fine-tune presented only as a 2025–2026 abstract cannot be excluded.

What has changed since the group's September 2026 memo is not the verdict on zero-shot generalist embeddings — VCBench, the August zero-shot benchmark and scTOP reinforce it — but three things that bear on building something ovary-specific. First, the task map is now sharp enough to say where a dedicated model could add value: perturbation and condition-effect prediction given in-context data, low-supervision annotation of rare states, and gene-program embeddings, rather than integration or clustering. Second, Arc shipped a complete, GPU-accelerated evaluation contract (`cell-eval2` 0.21.0, released 2026-11-03) with baseline-scaled, replicate-ceilinged metrics that any lab can run on its own data [V] ([PyPI cell-eval2](https://pypi.org/project/cell-eval2/); [ArcInstitute/cell-eval2](https://github.com/ArcInstitute/cell-eval2)). Third, the corpora, licences and training recipes needed for continued pretraining of a mid-sized mouse-native model are now public (scPRINT-2's pretraining documentation and SLURM recipe, Tahoe-x1's Composer/FSDP stack and 70 M config, scBaseCount's ovary SRA samples with perturbation metadata), and the Cell Ontology gained a stage-resolved granulosa, theca and luteal vocabulary between April 2024 and June 2026 that did not exist when most ovary atlases were annotated [V] ([scPRINT-2 pretrain.md](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/cantinilab/scPRINT-2/main/docs/pretrain.md); [tahoe-x1 README](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/tahoebio/tahoe-x1/main/README.md); [CL #2683](https://github.com/obophenotype/cell-ontology/issues/2683)).

## Census holds 0.4 % human and 0.08 % mouse ovary, half of it tumour

Every number in this section was computed by opening the public Census SOMA object directly (`s3://cellxgene-census-public-us-west-2/cell-census/<version>/soma/`) and the Arc GCS bucket (`gs://arc-institute-virtual-cell-atlas`) with TileDB-SOMA and pandas; the machine-readable tables are saved as `tissue_general_homo_sapiens_2025-11-08.csv`, `tissue_general_mus_musculus_2025-11-08.csv`, `ovary_across_releases.csv`, `repro_datasets_homo_sapiens_2025-11-08.csv`, `repro_datasets_mus_musculus_2025-11-08.csv`, `tahoe_cell_lines.csv` and `scbasecount_ovary_summary.csv` next to the notes [M] ([S3 bucket listing](https://cellxgene-census-public-us-west-2.s3.us-west-2.amazonaws.com/?list-type=2&prefix=cell-census/&delimiter=/); [LTS 2025-11-08 prefix](https://cellxgene-census-public-us-west-2.s3.us-west-2.amazonaws.com/?list-type=2&prefix=cell-census/2025-11-08/&delimiter=/)). The Census docs list LTS releases 2025-11-08, 2025-01-30, 2024-07-01, 2023-12-15, 2023-07-25 and 2023-05-15 and never mention 2026; a listing with prefix `cell-census/2026` returned zero keys on 2026-11-03, and the newest weekly build (2025-11-17, schema 2.4.0) has identical ovary numbers, so LTS 2025-11-08 is the latest corpus any 2026 model could have drawn on [M/V] ([Census release docs](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/chanzuckerberg/cellxgene-census/main/docs/cellxgene_census_docsite_data_release_info.md); [Census schema docs](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/chanzuckerberg/cellxgene-census/main/docs/cellxgene_census_docsite_schema.md); [Census analysis docs](https://chanzuckerberg.github.io/cellxgene-census/notebooks/analysis_demo/comp_bio_census_info.html)). Three features of this gap matter more than the headline percentages. Volume is the least of them: ovary at 0.08–0.4 % is roughly 70–200× smaller than brain or blood, but kidney, liver and lung are also an order of magnitude smaller than brain and nobody calls them under-modelled. Composition is worse: **half of human ovary cells are high-grade serous tumour cells, and the follicular compartment that defines the organ — granulosa, theca, oocyte — is 3.2 % of primary human ovary cells, with 18 primary human oocytes and 237 mouse oocytes in the entire corpus** [M]. Biological coverage is worst: the mouse data consist of one strain at two ages with no estrous staging, no ages beyond 9 months, no nuclei, no perturbations and no spatial data, and the human data carry no cycle-phase annotation and skew to post-menopausal and cancer donors [M/I]. A generalist pretrained on these corpora has therefore seen essentially no cycling ovary, no ovulation, no luteal regression, no reproductive ageing beyond mid-life and no ovarian response to any intervention — which is exactly the biology an ovary model would be built to predict.

The 2026 Challenge, launched 2026-08-20 and closing 2026-11-05 23:59 UTC with results due late November, changes the task from unseen perturbations in a known line to **zero-shot prediction in unseen cellular contexts**. Arc ran identical CRISPRi Perturb-seq (10x Flex) in six cancer cell lines from different tissues, profiled 30 M+ cells, selected 300 targets per line with ≥80 % median knockdown, and downsampled every perturbation to exactly 400 cells at a median depth of 20,000 UMI on a common axis of 18,533 genes; participants receive, per context, only 18,400 non-targeting control cells (46 guides × 400) and the list of 300 target gene IDs — "there is no Challenge training set this year" — for validation contexts A/B/C and then final contexts D/E/F with a new 300-gene panel. Prizes total $175,000 ($100 K / $50 K / $25 K, cash plus NVIDIA Brev credits) and open weights or code are not required [S/V] ([Arc 2026 announcement](https://arcinstitute.org/news/virtual-cell-challenge-2026); [Arc X launch post](https://x.com/arcinstitute/status/2090492640936722677); [pre-registration post](https://x.com/arcinstitute/status/2084700768821784806); [sponsors post](https://x.com/arcinstitute/status/2090492646917861639); [data post](https://x.com/arcinstitute/status/2106085356051509487); [Arc "Behind the data" 2026](https://arcinstitute.org/news/behind-the-data-virtual-cell-challenge-2026); [Arc "Behind the data" 2025](https://arcinstitute.org/news/behind-the-data-virtual-cell-challenge); [Cell 2026 commentary](https://www.cell.com/cell/fulltext/S0092-8674(26)00931-1); [QxMD record](https://read.qxmd.com/read/42648290/virtual-cell-challenge-2026-benchmarking-zero-shot-generalization-across-cellular-contexts); [10x "30-million-cell gauntlet"](https://www.10xgenomics.com/peer-perspectives/arcinstitute); [virtualcellchallenge.org](https://virtualcellchallenge.org/); [technooptimist](https://technooptimist.io/ai/virtual-cell-challenge); [rpinho/vcc2026](https://github.com/rpinho/vcc2026); [PUBLISH_CHECK.md](https://github.com/rpinho/vcc2026/blob/main/PUBLISH_CHECK.md); [AtlasShift](https://github.com/kaipengm2/Virtual-Cell-Challenge-2026); [vcc2026-metrics-brief.md](https://github.com/ArcInstitute/cell-eval2/blob/main/docs/vcc2026_metrics/vcc2026-metrics-brief.md); [vcc2026-metrics.md](https://github.com/ArcInstitute/cell-eval2/blob/main/docs/vcc2026_metrics/vcc2026-metrics.md); [Virtual_Cell README](../README.md)). The upload contract is strict: one file for all three contexts with `obs["context"]`, `target_gene` symbols, exactly the perturbations in `pert_counts.csv`, no control rows ("the control cells supplied with the phase are model inputs only and must not be copied into the prediction"), exactly 400 cells per perturbation, exactly 18,533 genes in order, raw integer counts, and at most 4.75 billion stored entries, so a complete submission is 360,000 cells of integer counts and a generative or sampling step is mandatory even for pseudobulk-style models [V]. Tooling as of 2026-11-03: `cell-eval2` 0.21.0 (released that day; 0.16.0 at launch), `vcc-cli` 0.3.0, `cell-eval` 0.8.2, `arc-state` 0.11.1 and `cell-load` 0.10.4 [V] ([PyPI cell-eval2](https://pypi.org/project/cell-eval2/); [PyPI vcc-cli](https://pypi.org/project/vcc-cli/); [PyPI arc-state](https://pypi.org/project/arc-state/); [PyPI cell-load](https://pypi.org/project/cell-load/)).

The scoring rule is the part worth copying. Every one of the six metrics is rescaled as **s = (u − b) / (r − b)**, where u is the submission's panel aggregate, b is the context's mean-perturbation-response baseline (an equal-weight average, over constructs passing a cell-count and knockdown-efficiency filter, of the per-construct mean count vector, assigned identically to every perturbation and scored as an ordinary submission) and r is the reference compared with itself (cells split into two disjoint halves per perturbation and per control, one half scored against the other, averaged over five splits with `BASE_SEED = 0`, `N_SPLITS = 5`, each half using its own control cells). A context's score is the equal-weight mean of the six; the overall score is the unweighted mean over contexts; "values above 1 and below 0 both occur and are reported rather than clipped", except that the expression-error member is clamped to [0, 1] and the fold-change NMAE is floored at −6 [V] ([vcc2026-metrics-brief.md](https://github.com/ArcInstitute/cell-eval2/blob/main/docs/vcc2026_metrics/vcc2026-metrics-brief.md); [competition.py](https://github.com/ArcInstitute/cell-eval2/blob/main/src/cell_eval2/competition.py); [vcc2026.yaml](https://github.com/ArcInstitute/cell-eval2/blob/main/src/cell_eval2/configs/vcc2026.yaml); [catalog.py](https://github.com/ArcInstitute/cell-eval2/blob/main/src/cell_eval2/catalog.py); [docs/metrics.md](https://github.com/ArcInstitute/cell-eval2/blob/main/docs/metrics.md); [Arc X scoring post](https://x.com/arcinstitute/status/2090492642841039139)). All six metrics exclude target genes: `pds_cosine` removes all 300 panel targets from every distance, the other five remove the perturbation's own target, and the target row was "worth 6–11 % of the expression metric's range" before that rule [V].

**Table 4.1 — The `vcc2026` profile (cell-eval2 0.15.0 reference points on validation contexts A–C, rule_version 3; all [V])**

| Member | What it measures | How it is computed | Baseline b | Replicate r | What a trivial submission scores |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|

The shared DE table uses a per-gene two-sided Wilcoxon rank-sum on counts normalised to 10⁶ per cell, log2 fold change on arithmetic means with ε = 10⁻⁹, genes tested only if mean expression > 5 CPM in the reference control cells (so the gene set is identical for every submission), Benjamini–Hochberg within each perturbation at p_adj < 0.05, and the measured control as comparison group on both sides (`control_source: real`) — so a model's own control reconstruction is never scored [V]. Because r is estimated at half depth, an exact reproduction of the reference scores above 1 (1.03–1.17 on PDS, 1.000 on MSE, 1.51–1.72 on fidelity, 1.02–1.05 on reach, 2.48–2.85 on Jaccard, 1.58–1.75 on NMAE), and the seed noise of r is 0.2–4.8 % of the span depending on the member [V]. Three in-competition anti-gaming fixes (rule_version 2 → 3, shipped 2026-08-18/19) are instructive for anyone building their own benchmark: spiking the panel's other targets had bought +0.49 to +0.57 of PDS member score "on no information beyond the target list", a jackknife-credit exploit took +0.1389 of a 0.2295 overall score, and a gene's mere presence in the DE table disclosed its sign ("P(real log2FC > 0) = 1.000000 over 26,373 / 33,969 / 26,839 such rows"), letting a control-paste submission gain +0.1057 [V] ([competition.py](https://github.com/ArcInstitute/cell-eval2/blob/main/src/cell_eval2/competition.py)). On the live validation leaderboard a participant recorded rank 463 of 1,580 at 0.16438 (components pds 0.706, mse 0.061, nmae 0.116, fid −0.066, reach 0.186, jac −0.016) and observed that the MSE component for entries ranked 10–50 had a median of about 0.44; AtlasShift reported #82 at 0.1546 on 2026-09-10 [V] ([rpinho/vcc2026](https://github.com/rpinho/vcc2026); [AtlasShift](https://github.com/kaipengm2/Virtual-Cell-Challenge-2026)). Four of six metrics are DE-based and the baselines for reach and Jaccard are near zero, so recovering the identity and direction of the top responding genes dominates the achievable score, while fold-change magnitude and integer-count error are the hardest terms [I].

Reusing this as an evaluation standard for an ovary model requires no Challenge entry, because every ingredient is public: `cell-eval2 run --preset vcc2026` and `EvalConfig.from_preset("vcc2026")` reproduce competition scoring, `cell-eval run --ceiling` performs the split-half self-comparison with the Spearman–Brown correction r' = 2r/(1 + r) for bounded reliability metrics, and the reference bundle holding b and r for a new dataset is built from the lab's own real data with five split-half seeds [V] ([cell-eval2 README](https://github.com/ArcInstitute/cell-eval2); [cell-eval README](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/ArcInstitute/cell-eval/main/README.md); [cell-eval _evaluator](https://github.com/ArcInstitute/cell-eval)). The ovary translation of the 2026 design is to hold out whole contexts rather than perturbations: a context is an ovarian cell type (granulosa, theca, stroma, luteal, oocyte), a condition (estrous stage, age, oophorectomy versus sham) or a time point (hours post-hCG), the model receives only control profiles of the held-out context, and it must emit integer-count cells whose pseudobulk delta, DE set, DE directions and fold changes beat the context-mean baseline on the same 0–1 scale [I]. Several constraints follow directly from the spec. Groups need enough cells for a split-half replicate — Arc uses 400 cells per perturbation and 18,400 controls per context, and the DE gate needs ≥10 significant genes for NMAE — so an ovary benchmark built on a 2-vs-2-mouse design must define its replicate ceiling within group across animals and accept that rare compartments (oocytes, luteal cells) will often fall below the gate. Raw counts, not log-normalised values, are required, which favours count-likelihood models and makes mixing nuclei with cells a modelling choice rather than a preprocessing one. And the scorer's exclusion of target genes has an ovary analogue: hormone time courses must exclude the immediate-early genes that define the time point, or the "target list" leaks the answer. The 2025-style `vcc` profile (MAE, PDS-L1, DE overlap, clipped at zero) should not be reused, since Arc's own analysis shows it rewarded mean-shift accuracy over distributional realism and allowed metric-by-metric gaming [I].
## Cross-context transfer is mostly a data problem, and ovary has no Perturb-seq

What cross-context perturbation methods actually achieve in late 2026 is narrower than the "virtual cell" framing suggests, and the gap between chemical and genetic transfer is the most decision-relevant fact. STATE pairs a set-based State Transition model trained on more than 100 million perturbed cells across 70 contexts with a BERT-like State Embedding model trained on 167 million observational human cells; context enters only through SE embeddings of control cells, and the transition model is conditioned on a perturbation embedding, with TOML `[zeroshot]` splits that hold out entire cell types (the shipped example holds out `replogle.jurkat`) [V/S] ([ArcInstitute/state](https://github.com/ArcInstitute/state); [STATE README](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/ArcInstitute/state/main/README.md); [STATE bioRxiv v2](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2025.06.26.661135v2); [Arc X STATE post](https://x.com/arcinstitute/status/1937196910688997777); [fleetwood.dev primer](https://fleetwood.dev/posts/virtual-cell-challenge); [HyperAI](https://hyper.ai/en/news/40864); [BiopharmaTrend on Cell_Eval](https://biopharmatrend.com/post/1302-arc-institute-releases-its-first-virtual-cell-model-trained-on-270-million-cells)). Its reported margins over baselines on perturbation discrimination — **63 % on Tahoe-100M (50 drug-treated lines), 47 % on Parse-PBMC (cytokines), 5 % on Replogle-Nadig (four genetic-perturbation lines)** — and the *Cell* version's held-out-line comparison in which the context-mean baseline reached a correlation of about 0.2, the perturbation-mean baseline 0.31 and STATE significantly above both, are the strongest peer-reviewed evidence for cross-context transfer, and they say that transfer works where many contexts were trained on and barely works for genetic perturbations across few lines [S] ([Cell STATE paper](https://www.cell.com/cell/fulltext/S0092-8674(26)00921-9)). Tahoe-x1 reused STATE's transition hyperparameters and evaluated few-shot and zero-shot splits [S] ([Tahoe-x1 bioRxiv](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2025.10.23.683759v1.full.pdf); [DNAnexus](https://academy.dnanexus.com/public-datasets-on-the-dnanexus-platform/ai-ml-experience-accelerator/tahoe-x1-foundation-model-for-perturbation-prediction)). The independent evidence is more sobering: ADAPERT found that on unseen-perturbation splits across K562, Jurkat, HepG2 and RPE1 the **simple perturbation-mean baseline achieved the highest Pearson-delta** "because most perturbation effects carry across cell lines" [S] ([arXiv 2602.18885](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2602.18885)); AROMA trained on K562, HepG2 and Jurkat and tested zero-shot on RPE1 with "only slight degradation" [S] ([arXiv 2604.20263](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2604.20263)); the "in-the-wild" benchmark found that models including linear ones "capture global transcriptional trends but fail to recover fine-grained perturbation-specific effects" in unseen contexts and had to retrain STATE from scratch because the public transition checkpoints "are specifically formulated for single-agent drug-dosage scenarios" [S] ([arXiv 2604.27646](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.27646); [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2604.27646)); Ahlmann-Eltze, Huber and Anders (*Nature Methods* 2025) showed five foundation models and two other deep models did not beat deliberately simple baselines, with an additive model winning on double perturbations and a linear model matching deep models on unseen single perturbations [S/V] ([Nature Methods s41592-025-02772-6](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41592-025-02772-6); [Nature Index record](https://www.nature.com/nature-index/article/10.1038/s41592-025-02772-6); [bioRxiv](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2024.09.16.613342v1); [const-ae repo](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/const-ae/linear_perturbation_prediction-Paper/main/README.md)); scPerturBench (27 methods, 29 datasets) reports persistent concerns for unseen contexts [S] ([Nature Methods s41592-025-02980-0](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41592-025-02980-0)); and earlier Perturb-seq benchmarks found scGPT comparable to a train-mean model and beaten by a CRISPR-informed random forest, with scGPT and scFoundation underperforming train-mean across four datasets [S] ([Bioinformatics btaf317](https://academic.oup.com/bioinformatics/article/41/6/btaf317/8142305); [BMC Genomics](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s12864-025-11600-2); [Csendes et al.](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/391056964_Benchmarking_foundation_cell_models_for_post-perturbation_RNA-seq_prediction); [PertEval-scFM](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2024.10.02.616248.full.pdf); ["far from straightforward"](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.64898/2026.02.14.705879v1.full); [tabular FMs](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/biorxiv/early/2026/07/01/2026.06.28.735106.full.pdf); [Open Problems task](https://github.com/openproblems-bio/task_perturbation_prediction); [Open Problems leaderboard](https://openproblems.bio/results/perturbation_prediction); [Kernfeld benchmarks](https://ekernf01.github.io/perturbation-benchmarks)). Other 2026 approaches — CellFlow and CFM-GP (flow matching with condition embeddings), Lingshu-Cell (masked discrete diffusion), X-Cell Mini (a 55 M-parameter diffusion LM on 25.6 M perturbed cells claiming 5× higher Pearson Δ than the next-best method on held-out perturbations, with weights "coming soon"), module-inductive gene representations, STRAND sequence-conditioned transport, and the older scGen latent arithmetic — are preprints without independent held-out-context validation [S/V] ([CellFlow](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2025.04.11.648220v1); [CFM-GP](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.08312v3); [Lingshu-Cell](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.25240v1); [X-Cell](https://github.com/Xaira-Therapeutics/X-Cell); [Beyond Independent Genes](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2602.04901); [STRAND](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2602.10156); [scGen](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/478503.full.pdf); [yashnil/virtual-cell-generalization](https://github.com/yashnil/virtual-cell-generalization)).

The 2026 Challenge participants' public post-mortems say the same thing from the inside. AtlasShift transferred measured responses from five public datasets (K562 GWPS, HCT116 and HEK293T from X-Atlas/Orion, H1 2025, CD4 T cells; ≈117 GB of downloads, ≈10 minutes of inference) with a weighted ensemble, target-context controls and a promoter-neighbour correction, then sampled integer counts matching mean CPM and pseudobulk, and reached the top 100 [V] ([AtlasShift](https://github.com/kaipengm2/Virtual-Cell-Challenge-2026)). The rpinho retrospective is blunt: "What helps is in-context data. Other knockdowns measured in the same cell type lifted alignment from 0.115 to 0.42"; "With public data, predicting a new cell line is mostly a data problem. It is not a modelling problem"; "Spend the time on expression error"; and "More DE calls are not a free improvement" because reach rises while fidelity and Jaccard fall [V] ([rpinho/vcc2026 RETRO and LESSONS](https://github.com/rpinho/vcc2026)). Team Outlier's third place in 2025 with TransPert — pooling Wilcoxon and pseudobulk summaries from reference lines with similarity-aware aggregation and a global linear scaling — was already this strategy [S]. The dominant practical recipe for an unseen context is therefore retrieval and transfer: look up each perturbation's measured signature in public screens, weight sources by similarity of their controls to the new context's controls, apply the delta to the new control pseudobulk, and sample integer counts with control-matched dispersion; learned set-attention models must do the same implicitly [I]. Every public genetic-perturbation atlas used by top teams — K562, RPE1, HCT116, HEK293T, Jurkat, HepG2, hPSC/H1, primary CD4 T cells, Tahoe's 50 lines — is a cancer line, an immortalised line or blood, so an ovary model cannot borrow in-context knockdown signatures and would depend on cross-context transfer plus its own perturbation data [I] ([PRiMeFlow corpus](https://github.com/altoslabs/primeflow)). Human-to-mouse transfer exists only via orthology mapping: CytoWave (NeurIPS 2026) pools cross-species perturbation data by orthology with dataset-held-out evaluation, EVA fine-tunes on mouse and tests on human via scGPT ortholog mapping, Mix-Geneformer evaluates zero-shot human↔mouse in-silico perturbation, and Icebear predicts human AD-versus-control changes from mouse data; none is in ovary and none was scored against a context-mean baseline [S] ([CytoWave](https://neurips.cc/virtual/2026/poster/149093); [EVA arXiv 2602.10168](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2602.10168); [Mix-Geneformer](https://web3.arxiv.org/abs/2507.07454); [Icebear](https://storage.prod.researchhub.com/uploads/papers/2025/11/22/2023.10.19.563173v1.full.pdf)).

Ovary "perturbation-like" data are real but fragmented and almost entirely hormonal, ageing or genetic rather than pooled screens. **No Perturb-seq or CROP-seq screen in granulosa cells or granulosa lines (KGN, COV434, HGrC1) was found**; search 12 returned only generic Perturb-seq methods, while in-vivo Perturb-seq precedents exist in mouse brain (autism risk genes, 22q11.2 AAV-Perturb-seq, cortical development, a 2025 *Nature Protocols* method, spatial Perturb-seq with Stereo-seq) and liver (Perturb-Multimodal, *Cell* 2025) [S] ([Perturb-seq Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Perturb-seq); [Replogle direct-capture](https://collaborate.princeton.edu/en/publications/combinatorial-single-cell-crispr-screens-by-direct-guide-rna-capt/); [Science 2020](https://www.science.org/doi/10.1126/science.aaz6063); [bioRxiv cortical](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2023.09.18.558077.full.pdf); [PMC10567566](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC10567566/); [Nat Protoc](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41596-024-01128-2); [Perturb-Multimodal](https://www.cell.com/cell/fulltext/S0092-8674(25)00572-0); [Nat Commun spatial Perturb-seq](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-026-69677-6)). The usable condition-structured datasets are the hCG/PMSG ovulation time courses (GSE294534 at 0/4/12 h with paired MERFISH; the *Nature Cell Biology* 2024 ex-vivo 0/3/6/9/12 h series; Mantri's eight-stage Slide-seq GSE240271; the scBaseCount 0/3/6/9/12 h follicle series SRX23566711–38 with in-vitro arms; Park's 6 h eCG/hCG sample GSM4306338; Morris 2022's PMSG/hCG 8 h arm), the ageing and menopause cohorts (GSE232309 3 vs 9 months; GSE267729 estropausal transition; GSE290742 and PRJNA863443 including the VCD chemical-menopause model; E-MTAB-12889; the Slide-seq 610 K-spot ageing atlas), genetic models (ERα KO, Cacna2d3 KO with 16,705 cells, Trim28 KO in SRA), diet and drug exposures in SRA (metformin 350 mg/kg/day in aged fibrotic ovary, doxorubicin 3 mg/kg ± AMH 0.75 mg/kg, melatonin + DEHP, LPS, high-fat diet), and drug-treated ovarian-cancer lines and PDX (OVCAR3, OVCAR5, Kuramochi, talazoparib series, bexmarilimab, checkpoint bispecifics) [M/S]. Bulk hCG granulosa time courses (GSE301074 from January 2026, GSE167939, PRJNA705643) exist but are not single-cell [S] ([OmicsDI GSE301074](https://www.omicsdi.org/dataset/geo/GSE301074); [GSE167939](https://www.omicsdi.org/dataset/geo/GSE167939); [PRJNA705643](https://omicsdi.org/dataset/project/PRJNA705643)). Rapamycin and 30 % caloric restriction have only bulk data (GeroScience 2019) [S] ([Springer](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11357-019-00087-x)); cyclophosphamide and cisplatin injury in mouse is bulk granulosa RNA-seq or histology, and the one human chemotherapy-exposed cortex study is a thesis whose single-cell content is unconfirmed [S] ([BMC Pharmacol Toxicol](https://bmcpharmacoltoxicol.biomedcentral.com/track/pdf/10.1186/s40360-020-00468-5); [prepubertal somatic cells bioRxiv](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2020.06.23.167783v1.full); [KI thesis](https://openarchive.ki.se/articles/thesis/The_human_ovary_a_characterization_of_cell_types_and_adverse_ovarian_side_effects_of_chemotherapy/26898835/1)); human POI single-cell data are peripheral blood, not ovary [S] ([Front Immunol 2023](https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fimmu.2023.1297484/full)); the goat follicular-fluid hCG series is the only non-mouse hormonal time course [S]. Compensatory ovulation after unilateral ovariectomy — the group's own experimental paradigm — has **no single-cell dataset anywhere**; the literature is rat contralateral hypertrophy histology, inhibin mRNA in newly recruited follicles, a pig oviduct microarray and mouse compensation timing from day 5–6 [S] ([J Ovarian Res 2016](https://ovarianresearch.biomedcentral.com/track/pdf/10.1186/s13048-016-0252-9); [Woodruff lab inhibin](https://woodrufflab.org/publication/unilateral-ovariectomy-increases-inhibin-messenger-ribonucleic-acid-levels-in-newly-recruited-follicles-2/); [J Hum Reprod Sci 2013](https://doaj.org/article/cda462e72e9b4d06837f7edbb71275dc)). Nor is there a corpus-luteum regression atlas in isolation (only bulk cow, dog, cat and macaque studies) [S] ([ADSA 2019](https://m.adsa.org/2019/abs/t/79257); [canine PMC5618722](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC5618722/)). Two consequences follow: an ovary perturbation benchmark must be assembled from GEO and SRA directly because nothing in Census or the Arc atlas qualifies, and the group's oophorectomy data, together with the planned 120-day cohort, would be first-of-its-kind and therefore cannot be scored against public ground truth — it can only serve as a held-out context whose internal replicate structure sets the ceiling [I]. The nearest "in-distribution" anchors for an ovarian context in Tahoe-100M are the endometrial (HEC-1-A, AN3 CA) and cervical (C-33 A) carcinoma lines, by line identity rather than by any Tahoe metadata [I]; LINCS L1000 and PRISM include ovarian cancer lines in their broader panels, but that is unverified background and these are bulk resources [I].

## Permissive, mouse-native bases with training code exist, and scale buys little here

The choice between continued pretraining and from-scratch training is constrained first by licences and training-code availability, which the researchers verified by reading LICENSE files and READMEs directly, and second by what the scaling literature says about a corpus of one to a few million cells.

The scaling literature places an ovary corpus squarely in the regime where capacity buys little. Kendiukhov's masked-reconstruction study found clean power-law scaling (α = 0.234, R² = 0.823, irreducible loss floor c ≈ 1.44) only in a data-rich regime of 200,000 cells × 512 HVGs, and "negligible scaling (α ≈ 0.009, R² ≈ 0.02)" at 10,000 cells × 1,024 genes, "indicating that model capacity is not the binding constraint when data are scarce" [S] ([arXiv 2602.15253](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.15253)); TEDDY reports predictable improvement with data and parameters up to 116 M cells and 400 M parameters [S] ([arXiv 2503.03485](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2503.03485)); "Scaling recipes" finds power-law dependence of pretraining loss on model size whose regularity "differ[s] between model formulations" [S] ([bioRxiv 2026.08.31.747783](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.64898/2026.08.31.747783v1); [Sciety listing](https://sciety.org/articles/activity/10.64898/2026.08.31.747783)); but DenAdel's downstream result — saturation at a fraction of corpus size, "no clear data scaling laws", a 95 %-of-max "learning saturation point" reached early for cell-type classification and batch integration — is the one that concerns a lab with a few million cells [S/V] ([Nature Methods](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41592-026-03120-y); [scFM-dataselection downsampling README](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/microsoft/scFM-dataselection/main/downsampling/README.md); [HF microsoft/scFM-dataselection](https://huggingface.co/microsoft/scFM-dataselection); [PLOS Comp Bio Geneformer scale study](https://journals.plos.org/ploscompbiol/article?id=10.1371/journal.pcbi.1013701)). Richter et al. (*Nature Machine Intelligence* 2025) add that self-supervised pretraining with simple fully connected masked autoencoders on scTab helps mainly in transfer to auxiliary or novel data and that "self-supervised pre-training on the same dataset as the fine-tuning does not yield improvement" [S] ([bioRxiv 2024.02.16.580624](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2024.02.16.580624.full.pdf)). Every documented continued-pretraining or from-scratch specialisation (Geneformer CLcancer at ~14 M cells, scGPT-spatial at 30 M spots, Nephrobase at 39.5 M, OKR-CELL at 32 M) sits at ≥10 M cells, five to forty times what a curated ovary corpus will contain, so gains at ovary scale should be expected to be modest and task-specific, which is exactly what the harmonised benchmark found for continual pretraining in general [I].

Several engineering choices follow from the notes rather than from taste. Gene vocabulary: Ensembl assigns 21,428 mouse orthologs to human symbols but only 16,573 (77 %) uniquely, 17,504 (82 %) after sequence-based rescue, so a strict one-to-one vocabulary (the Geneformer and scGPT route) discards roughly a fifth of mouse genes, whereas ESM2 protein-embedding gene tokens (UCE, TranscriptFormer, SATURN's "macrogenes", scPRINT-2's precomputed embeddings, STATE-SE's optional embedding dict) let ovary-specific or mouse-specific genes be added by computing an embedding rather than retraining a table [S/V] ([OrthoIntegrate PMC10993718](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC10993718/); [SATURN PMC11310084](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC11310084/)). Modality: the field encodes suspension type and assay as tokens (Nicheformer's `TENX_CELL`/`TENX_NUCLEUS`, scPRINT-2's assay classes) rather than by gene filtering, no scFM paper quantifies the effect of mixing nuclei and cells or applies Malat1/intronic corrections inside pretraining, and spot-level Visium or Visium HD data are excluded from pretraining by Nicheformer, Novae, HEIST and scPRINT-2 (which drops EFO:0010961) and included only by scGPT-spatial through a protocol-aware mixture-of-experts decoder and a neighbourhood objective, while Nephrobase pretrains jointly on sc, sn, ATAC and spatial [V/S] ([HEIST](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2506.11152); [CVPR 2026 supplement](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/CVPR2026F/supplemental/Zhu_Spatial_Transcriptomics_as_CVPRF_2026_supplemental.pdf); [scMamba](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.13191v1); [OSCA snRNA chapter](https://bioconductor.org/books/3.16/OSCA.advanced/single-nuclei-rna-seq-processing.html); [Biostars Malat1](https://biostars.org/p/9593748/)). A count-likelihood objective on raw counts (scPRINT-2, TranscriptFormer, scVI) avoids a binning or ranking step that interacts badly with the depth differences between nuclei, cells and 8-µm bins, and it is also what the `vcc2026` scorer consumes [I]. Cross-species: the only direct evidence that adding human data helps a mouse task is Mix-Geneformer's 95.8 % versus 94.9 % on mouse kidney, against Souza and Mehta's counter-evidence that linear pipelines beat TranscriptFormer's reported cross-species numbers, so "mouse-only versus mouse + human" should be a pre-registered ablation at matched compute rather than an assumption [S/I] ([Mix-Geneformer html](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.07454v1); [scTOP html](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.16696)). Finally, no controlled comparison of rank-value, binned and count tokenisation at matched compute exists, and no published pre-registration template or kill rule for an scFM project was found [I].

Put together, the notes support one of two designs and argue against a third. Continued pretraining of a mid-sized mouse-native base — scPRINT-2 medium (90 M) is the most direct fit because it is mouse-native, count-based, uses ESM-style gene embeddings, ships the pretraining recipe and already contains the public ovary data, at the price of GPL-3 — or a from-scratch ≤30 M model in the scPRINT-2 or Tahoe-x1 70 M configuration on a 1–2 M-cell ovary corpus, which Kendiukhov's data-limited regime says would lose little to a larger model and which the maintainers' own SLURM example puts at a few days on four 40 GB GPUs, are both defensible [I]. A large from-scratch model is not: at ovary scale there is no evidence that capacity helps, and the only organ-specific from-scratch exemplar (Nephrobase, 39.5 M cells, 500 M and 1 B variants) had twenty times the data [I]. STATE-SE is usable for non-commercial research but its licence makes any fine-tuned ovary weights a non-commercial derivative and excludes industry-sponsored projects; scFoundation weights carry similar terms [V]. In every case scVI/scANVI (and a Richter-style masked-autoencoder MLP) must be the primary baseline, because the 2026 benchmarks say that is the model to beat, and the harmonised benchmark's recommendation — judge "biological generalisation, interpretability and perturbation-grounded validity, not scale or leaderboard performance alone" — is the right framing for a reviewer [S] ([arXiv 2607.17227](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2607.17227)).

## The ovary tools gap is real too: no reference, no clock, no crosswalk

Beyond corpora and models, the ovary lacks the reference infrastructure that brain, lung and kidney take for granted. The CellTypist built-in model list mirrored by the Galaxy tool-shed includes no ovary or gonad model (its adult models are cortex, islet, skin, vascular, breast, heart, liver, hippocampus), and the live `celltypist.org/models` list could not be fetched [S] ([Galaxy celltypist_models.loc](https://testtoolshed.g2.bx.psu.edu/repos/iuc/data_manager_celltypist_models/comparison/default/test-data/celltypist_models.loc); [CellTypist docs](https://docsearch.algolia.com/mcp/docs/repo/teichlab/celltypist)); the Sanger Human Hormone Cell Atlas bundle of 47 CellTypist models did not confirm ovary [S] ([Human Hormone Cell Atlas](https://hormonecellatlas.cellgeni.sanger.ac.uk/)). What exists is per-paper: a popV ensemble for Tabula Sapiens ovary on Hugging Face reporting ~0.97–0.98 accuracy for "stromal cell of ovary" [S] ([popV/tabula_sapiens_Ovary](https://huggingface.co/popV/tabula_sapiens_Ovary)); the Sanger `cellgeni/ovary` pipeline, which transfers labels by scANVI then BBKNN majority vote over 50 neighbours "because scANVI misannotated many reference cells", with its own granulosa scheme (`Granulosa_sq`, `_sq_atr`, `_sq_transitioning`, `_AMH_early`, `_AMH_ml`, `Stroma_InnCor`) [V] ([cellgeni/ovary](https://github.com/cellgeni/ovary)); and the Benayoun lab's CZI-funded cross-species ageing meta-analysis (human GSE255690, GSE202601, Tabula Sapiens; macaque GSE130664; mouse PRJNA863443, E-MTAB-12889, GSE232309, GSE290742; goat PRJNA1010653) whose vocabulary lives in per-dataset R scripts, not a reusable CellTypist or scArches object [V] ([Benayoun repo](https://github.com/BenayounLaboratory/Cross-species_ovarian_aging_analysis); [Genes & Dev review](https://genesdev.cshlp.org/content/39/15-16/975.short); [Mouse_Menopause_Models](https://github.com/BenayounLaboratory/Mouse_Menopause_Models)). The HCA Reproduction Network says its atlases "are still under development" [S] ([HCA Reproduction Network](https://www.humancellatlas.org/biological-networks/reproduction-biological-network/); [CZI seed network](https://chanzuckerberg.com/science/programs-resources/cell-science/seednetworks/human-cell-atlas-of-the-female-reproductive-system/); [MIDAS](https://midas.umich.edu/?p=1041); [CZI pediatric ovary](https://chanzuckerberg.com/science/programs-resources/cell-science/pediatric-networks/a-single-cell-map-of-the-pediatric-ovary-in-3d/); [Sanger reproductive tract](https://www.eurekalert.org/news-releases/1110097)).

Label inconsistency is documented rather than suspected. An eLife reviewer of Morris 2022 wrote that "the description of the mesenchyme cell subpopulations is confusing as the authors do not use consistent terminology" and the authors renamed "Theca 1/2" and "stroma 1/2" to early theca, steroidogenic theca and smooth muscle; Isola 2024 instead split stroma into fibroblast-like, smooth muscle, pericyte, early TC and TC; Wu/Tang report "three granulosa subtypes and five theca and stromal subtypes" without ontology mapping; the AJOG expert review notes marker genes "vary between studies"; PreMeno unified labels via Cell Ontology but admits "the lack of automated protocol for this process"; and the caprine granulosa classifier paper states that "no standardized metric or computational model currently exists to classify GC subpopulations at the single-cell level" [V/S] ([CL #2602](https://github.com/obophenotype/cell-ontology/issues/2602); [PMC12415129](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC12415129/); [PLoS ONE 2016 SVM](https://doaj.org/article/7a015805e0ab469e94687adc438ecf41)). The fix that now exists is the Cell Ontology: between April 2024 and June 2026, HuBMAP's ovary ASCT+B work and a CZI-cellxgene "Reproductive System Jamboree" added or restructured at least 14 ovary terms — pre-granulosa (CL:4033066) and mural granulosa (CL:4033067), squamous and cuboidal granulosa, cumulus and mural as subclasses of granulosa, atretic granulosa and atretic theca, pre-theca, interna and externa theca, steroidogenic stromal cell ("Enpp2− interstitial cells"), early (Parm1) and late (Akr1c18) luteal cells replacing small/large, luteal cells removed from under stroma, and follicular cell of ovary asserted as epithelial — most of them anchored to mouse markers from Morris 2022 [V] ([CL #2360](https://github.com/obophenotype/cell-ontology/issues/2360); [#2527](https://github.com/obophenotype/cell-ontology/issues/2527); [#2528](https://github.com/obophenotype/cell-ontology/issues/2528); [#2792](https://github.com/obophenotype/cell-ontology/issues/2792); [#2728](https://github.com/obophenotype/cell-ontology/issues/2728); [#2732](https://github.com/obophenotype/cell-ontology/issues/2732); [#2683](https://github.com/obophenotype/cell-ontology/issues/2683); [#2976](https://github.com/obophenotype/cell-ontology/issues/2976); [#2974](https://github.com/obophenotype/cell-ontology/issues/2974); [#2978](https://github.com/obophenotype/cell-ontology/issues/2978); [#2980](https://github.com/obophenotype/cell-ontology/issues/2980); [#2999](https://github.com/obophenotype/cell-ontology/issues/2999); [#3650](https://github.com/obophenotype/cell-ontology/issues/3650); [CL issue list](https://github.com/obophenotype/cell-ontology/issues); [FANTOM5 CL:0000501](https://fantom.gsc.riken.jp/5/sstar/CL:0000501)). **Inferences, and what they mean for this group.** First, the group's 50,000 oophorectomy/sham nuclei are a held-out test context, not a training corpus: they are a first-of-their-kind design with no public ground truth, would be among the larger mouse ovary snRNA-seq sets in existence (scBaseCount holds 86,346 mouse ovary nuclei in six experiments and Census holds none), and with two mice per arm the split-half replicate ceiling must be computed within group across animals, which the `cell-eval2` reference-bundle machinery supports but which will leave rare compartments below the ≥10-gene NMAE gate; the planned 120-day cohort doubles the number of contexts and should be designed with ≥400 cells per compartment per arm in mind. Second, the dedicated-model opportunity is not "an ovary embedding" — the memo's conclusion stands and the 2026 benchmarks reinforce it — but a model that has seen the cycling, ovulating, ageing and perturbed ovary that the generalists have not, evaluated on condition-effect prediction in held-out contexts; the corpus to assemble is roughly 1–2 million mouse and human cells, nuclei and spots from Tables 3.1–3.4, re-processed from raw counts, with suspension-type tokens and a CL-aligned label crosswalk, and the group's existing CellTypist references against Morris 2022 and Isola 2024 are the seed of that crosswalk. Third, the base-model decision reduces to scPRINT-2 medium continued pretraining (mouse-native, count-based, training recipe shipped, GPL-3) versus a ≤30 M from-scratch model in the Tahoe-x1 or scPRINT-2 small configuration (Apache-2.0 or GPL-3), each at an extrapolated 200–500 A100-hours on one Biowulf 4×A100 node, with the untested scPRINT-2/UCE/TranscriptFormer embedding pilot repurposed as the zero-shot arm of the comparison and scVI/scANVI as the pre-registered primary baseline. Fourth, the Visium HD 8-µm bins should not enter core pretraining: either a separate spatial head with a neighbourhood loss in the scGPT-spatial style, or a Novae-style graph model over the transcript model's embeddings, with the McKey GSE307688 atlas (same platform, eight timepoints) as the public comparator. Fifth, the evaluation contract should be written before training: hold out whole atlases and one species direction for label transfer (macro-F1 and rare-type F1 against scANVI/scPoli), report scIB-style integration stratified by suspension type, score condition effects with `cell-eval2`'s six members anchored to the context-mean baseline and the split-half replicate, exclude time-point-defining genes the way Arc excludes targets, and pre-register a kill rule such as "abandon the transformer if it does not beat scVI/linear baselines by a pre-specified margin on at least two of three primary metrics on held-out atlases". Sixth, a data-size ablation (50 K / 200 K / 1 M / all cells, three seeds) and a mouse-only versus mouse+human ablation at matched compute are cheap at this model size and would each be a publishable contribution, because no organ-specific scaling curve and no controlled cross-species-benefit measurement exist.

The open questions that the notes could not close are whether any 2025–2026 conference abstract reports an ovary fine-tune of an scFM; whether the Nicheformer and Tahoe-x1 corpora contain ovary and mouse respectively; what the exact ovary composition of Genecorpus-104M, UCE's atlas and TranscriptFormer's pull is; whether a 2026 Census build exists under a different alias; what STATE's SE size and ST architecture are and whether Arc fielded a 2026 reference submission; whether a Generalist Prize exists in 2026; what the six 2026 cell lines are; the minimal in-domain corpus that yields a measurable gain for an organ-specific model; how much mixing nuclei and cells in pretraining costs or helps; and whether in-vivo AAV or intrabursal Perturb-seq in the mouse ovary — for which the liver and brain precedents are the feasibility anchors — is within the group's reach, since it is the single dataset that would turn an ovary model from a transfer exercise into something with in-context perturbation signal.

## Conclusion

The ovary gap is less a shortage of ovary data than a shortage of ovary data in the places models are built from, and that distinction decides the strategy. A generalist that has seen 0.1 % normal ovary, 18 human oocytes and no cycling, ovulating or perturbed ovary cannot be expected to predict ovarian condition effects, and the field's own benchmarks say that neither scale nor tissue restriction alone will fix that; what will is curating the >1 million cells, nuclei and spots that already exist in GEO and SRA into a count-level, ontology-aligned corpus, training a modest mouse-native model on it, and proving on held-out contexts that it beats scVI and the context-mean baseline on Arc's replicate-anchored scale. The Virtual Cell Challenge's lasting contribution to an ovary project is therefore not a leaderboard but an evaluation contract — baseline b, replicate r, raw counts, excluded targets, no clipping — that makes a small lab's claims legible to the same reviewers who are now sceptical of foundation models.

Two things would change this assessment. If the 2026 Challenge results due in late November show learned models clearly beating retrieval-and-transfer on genetic perturbations in unseen contexts, the case for a learned ovary transition model strengthens; the participant evidence so far says the opposite. And if the group can generate even a small in-vivo ovarian Perturb-seq or a denser hormonal time course, it would own the only in-context perturbation signal for the organ, which every 2026 result identifies as the thing that actually moves the score. Until then, the ovary model worth building is a well-curated, well-evaluated narrow model whose first products — the corpus, the Cell Ontology crosswalk, the held-out benchmark with its measured ceilings, and the organ-specific scaling curve — are valuable whether or not the transformer on top of them wins.
